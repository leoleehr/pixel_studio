OpenMeet the chord

Three or more notes sounding together make a chord

  • String 5 fret 3 C, string 4 fret 2 E, open string 3 G
  • String 2 fret 1 is C again, and open string 1 is E again
  • Five strings, but only three notes: C, E and G

C major triad = Do + Mi + Sol. In Chapter 7 you'll learn that a major triad stacks "a major third plus a minor third".

OpenThink about it

If you only know C, G, Am and F, how many songs can you play?

Too many to count. The progression these four chords make (I–V–vi–IV in C) is one of the most common chord loops in pop, from Western pop to Mandarin ballads. Change the order or the key and you can play a whole other batch of songs.

Build00:20–00:55 Chord diagrams

Chord diagrams: looking at the fretboard with the guitar stood upright

  • Vertical lines are strings: the far left is the thickest, string 6, and the far right the thinnest, string 1 (turned 90 degrees from tab)
  • Horizontal lines are frets: the thick line at the top is the nut, then frets 1, 2 and 3 going down
  • Dots show where to press, and the number inside is the finger: 1 index, 2 middle, 3 ring, 4 little
  • ○ means play the open string, × means skip it: when you strum, start from the first string that should be played

BuildYour first chord

C: start with the ring finger and place fingers from low to high

  • Order to place fingers: ring finger string 5 fret 3 → middle string 4 fret 2 → index string 2 fret 1
  • Common problem: if the pad of the middle finger touches string 3, or the index touches string 1, the note goes dead
BuildYour second chord

Am: only one finger different from C

C
Am
  • Index and middle fingers stay put
  • Only the ring finger moves, from string 5 fret 3 to string 3 fret 2
  • Strum Am from open string 5, skipping string 6
  • Listen closely: Am is a minor chord, darker than C with a touch of sadness
BuildYour third chord

G: spread your fingers and let all six strings ring

  • Middle finger string 6 fret 3, index string 5 fret 2, ring finger string 1 fret 3
  • Drop your wrist and keep your palm off the neck so your fingers can spread
BuildYour fourth chord

Small F: the index finger lies flat across two strings

  • Lay the index finger flat across strings 1 and 2 at fret 1, using the side nearer the thumb, where the bone is firmer and presses down more easily
  • Middle finger string 3 fret 2, ring finger string 4 fret 3; skip strings 6 and 5
  • The full barre F (all six strings) waits until Chapter 8

BuildEar training

Close your eyes and tell which chord you're hearing

  • Am is the only minor chord of the four and sounds the darkest
  • G uses all six strings and has the fullest bass
  • F uses only four strings and sounds thinnest and tightest
  • C is bright and settled, like coming home

Turn01:05–01:40 How to change chords

Get ready early and your chord changes get fast

ChangeFingers that stay putKey point
C → AmIndex, middleOnly the ring finger moves; the easiest pair
Am → FMiddle and ring fingers keep a similar shapeShift the whole hand one string toward strings 4 and 3, with the index finger flat
F → GNoneAll three fingers leave together; place the farthest, the middle finger (string 6), first
G → CNoneThe ring finger slides from string 1 to string 5, bringing the palm down with it

Close01:40–02:00 Common problems

Chord sounding dead? Flip the cards to find out why

The harder you press, the louder the chord
MythPosition matters more than strength: close to the fret, fingertips upright, and a light press makes it ring.
Muted notes mostly come from a finger touching the next string
FactIf your finger isn't upright enough, its pad presses on the next string. Pluck string by string once and you'll find where.
Longer nails make pressing easier
MythKeep your left-hand nails short so your fingertips can stand up.
Look at your hand first, then strum
MythYou can look at first, but the goal is to place your fingers with your eyes closed, leaving your eyes free to read the chart.
F is too hard, so it's fine to skip it for now
MythSmall F is your way into barre chords; get it now and Chapter 8 will be much easier.
If you can't change in time, strum the open strings
FactKeep the beat going and listeners will hardly notice. Your fingers will get faster with each change.
