Which part of a song do people usually remember most?

After hearing a new song, which part are you most likely to hum?

  1. Intro
  2. Verse
  3. Chorus
  4. Outro
The chorus usually carries the catchiest melody and lyrics in the song: the "hook". The chorus accompaniment has the most volume and density.

A note from the original handbook

After playing for a while you'll notice lots of songs are built the same way. Understand the structure and you can play different songs with the same approach.

A Verse

Tells the story: a lower melody, more words, and different lyrics each time.

B Chorus

Makes the point: the highest, most memorable melody, with lyrics that repeat.

BuildDesigning the accompaniment for each section

The verse makes room for the singer; the chorus lets the energy out

SectionJobSuggested accompaniment
IntroTell listeners the tempo and keyQuarter-note downstrokes, or one pass through the chorus chords
VerseGet the lyrics across clearlyBoom-chick-a or quarter notes, a bit quieter
Pre-chorusSlowly heat up8 beats, strumming more and more densely
ChorusThe song's high pointSyncopation or 16 beats, strumming all six strings
BridgeCreate contrastChange the strum, add stops, or just let chords ring
OutroWrap upSlow down, strum the last chord once and let it ring out fully

BuildThe capo

A capo: a nut you can move

  • Each fret higher raises every string by a semitone
  • The left hand uses its usual chord shapes, "treating the capo as fret 0"
  • Clamp it beside the fret and parallel to it for a clean sound
  • Chord charts often say "Capo 2, play C": capo at fret 2 with C shapes, actually in D
InteractiveCapo calculator
BuildWork it out

Shape plus capo equals the real key

The original handbook's music often shows "Key: F Play: C Capo: 5": use C shapes with the capo at fret 5 to play in F.

Rehearsal: play from start to finish without stopping

  • Round 1: play only, no singing yet, checking chords and sections
  • Round 2: sing quietly and find where you get stuck most
  • Round 3: treat it as the real performance and keep going even if you slip
  • In pairs, take turns as each other's audience and each give one suggestion
TurnBefore you go on

Five small tips for a more polished play-and-sing

1

Tune first

Tune before you go on, and have the capo on before you get up there.

2

Count yourself in

Count "1, 2, 3, 4" in your head before you start.

3

Keep going after a slip

Keep the right hand strumming and pick it back up in the next bar.

4

Look at the audience

Lift your head in the chorus so everyone can see your face.

5

Finish properly

Let the last chord ring right to the end, then thank everyone.
