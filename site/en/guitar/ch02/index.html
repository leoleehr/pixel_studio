OpenBeat warm-up

Beats: clap along with the metronome

① Clap and count

Clap once per beat and count "1, 2, 3, 4" out loud, clapping a bit louder on beat 1.

② Add half beats

Clap on the beat and say "&" in between: 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &.

③ Clap only the offbeats

Tap your foot on the beat and clap only on the "&". This is the most common groove in pop songs.

BPM means beats per minute. 60 BPM is exactly one beat a second, like the second hand on a clock.

What does the "4/4" you often see on guitar music mean?

Pick an answer now, and check it again after today's lesson.

  1. The song lasts four minutes
  2. Four chords played in turn four times
  3. 4 beats per bar, with the quarter note as one beat
  4. The tempo is 44 BPM
A time signature is like a fraction: the top 4 means "how many beats per bar" and the bottom 4 means "the quarter note counts as one beat". Part 3 today explains it in detail.

Why learn to read music

Chord charts, tab, numbered notation and staff notation are the shared language of musicians. If you can read music, you can learn new songs on your own.

Build00:20–00:55 Part 1: Pitch and the staff

The staff: the higher the position, the higher the note

Treble clef 𝄞

Also called the G clef: its curl centres on the second line, and that line is G (Sol).

Ledger lines

Notes beyond the five lines get short lines added above or below; middle C, for example, sits on the first ledger line below.

How guitar music is written

Guitar music is written an octave up: middle C on the page actually sounds an octave lower.

On the guitar, each fret is a half step and two frets are a whole step. So Mi→Fa and Si→Do are only one fret apart, and all other neighbouring notes are two frets apart.

BuildPart 2: Note values

Cut a bar in half, then in half again

Western music mostly divides in twos: each level down, the note is half as long and there are twice as many. From eighth notes down, a beam joins the notes in the same beat.

BuildRests and dots

Count rests just as carefully

A dot adds half again

A small dot to the right of a note adds half its length: dotted quarter = 1 + ½ = 1.5 beats; dotted half = 3 beats.

What rests mean

On a rest your hand stops and the sound is cut, while you keep counting in your head. Lots of songs stop suddenly at a key moment, and it sounds especially cool.

BuildPart 3: Time signatures

Time signatures set strong and weak beats: how many beats in a bar, and which note counts as one

2/4

Strong, weak. Marches, upbeat songs.

3/4

Strong, weak, weak. Waltzes, Happy Birthday.

4/4

Strong, weak, medium, weak. Most pop songs.

6/8

The eighth note counts as one beat, 6 beats per bar, with a rocking feel.

The top number is how many beats per bar; the bottom number is which note counts as one beat. 3/4 means "3 beats per bar, with the quarter note as one beat".

Which beat feels most natural for "Happy birthday to you"?

  1. 2/4: strong, weak
  2. 3/4: strong, weak, weak
  3. 4/4: strong, weak, medium, weak
"Happy | birthday | to you" groups as "strong, weak, weak", so it's in triple time. Chapter 5 teaches how to accompany triple time.

Which of these fills one bar of 4/4 exactly?

  1. 3 quarter notes
  2. 1 half note + 1 quarter note
  3. 6 eighth notes
  4. 1 half note + 2 quarter notes
A half note is 2 beats, plus two quarter notes at 1 beat each, makes 4 beats. The other options are only 3 beats.
BuildThe guitarist's map: tab

Six lines are the six strings, numbers are frets

  • The top line is the thinnest string, string 1; the bottom line is string 6
  • Numbers show which fret to press; 0 means open string
  • Numbers stacked on top of each other are played together (a chord)
  • Tab shows where, staff notation shows rhythm, and the two often appear together

Turn01:05–01:40 How the left hand presses the strings

Four keys to clean fretting

  • ① Close to the fret: press toward the right side of the fret space; it takes the least effort and buzzes least
  • ② Fingertips upright: use the area between tip and pad, with fingers close to 90 degrees to the fretboard so they clear the next string
  • ③ Thumb behind: on the middle line at the back of the neck, squeezing with the fingers like a clip, supporting from behind
  • ④ Just enough pressure: stop once the note sounds clean; pressing too hard tires you out and pushes the pitch sharp

TurnWarm-up (1)

Finger crawl 1-2-3-4: one finger per fret

Index on fret 5, middle on fret 6, ring on fret 7, little finger on fret 8. The right hand alternates "down, up" with the pick. When you press the next fret, keep the earlier fingers on the string.

TurnWarm-up (2)

Finger crawl 1-3-2-4: make each finger independent

Goal

Left-hand control and stretch, right-hand speed and accuracy.

How to speed up

Only add 4 BPM after two steady rounds in a row. The original handbook's 100 BPM is the final goal; start slow.

Move down the neck

Once it's comfortable, crawl from fret 1, where the frets are widest and the finger stretch does the most good.

InteractiveTeam contest · timed
TurnTeam contest

Finger-crawl relay

  • Each group lines up; each person takes one string, and the next person carries on with the next string
  • Metronome at 70 BPM; if the whole group stays steady across all six strings, 2 points
  • If someone stops or slips, pick up again from that person, 1 point
  • Round two goes up to 80 BPM; go for the group's top speed

TurnHow to think about practice

Myth or fact?

The faster you practise, the faster you improve
MythSlow and steady is how fingers remember. Speed comes naturally once you're steady.
Metronomes are only for beginners
MythProfessional musicians practise with a metronome every day before recording too.
Even 10 minutes of practice a day helps
FactShort practice every day works better than two hours once at the weekend.
You have to read staff notation to learn guitar
MythPop guitar mostly uses chord charts and tab; being able to read staff notation opens up a lot more music.
The little finger is too short, so you can skip it
MythThe little finger is your fourth main finger. Start training it now and later chords and scales will be much easier.
Rest when your fingers hurt
FactWhen soreness turns into a sharp pain, rest a few minutes and practise again tomorrow; your fingertips will slowly build calluses.
