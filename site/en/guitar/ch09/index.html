OpenThink about it

In "C/G" on a chord chart, what does the G after the slash mean?

Choose the right meaning

  1. Alternate between C and G chords
  2. Play a C chord with G as the lowest note
  3. Play the C chord in the key of G
  4. Play C, then switch to G
The chord comes before the slash and the bass after it; these are called "slash chords". C/G is read "C over G".

The original handbook's Chapter 3

"The murmur of the bass": as well as being the chord's root, the bass can substitute and move, as if a line were threading the chords together.

Build00:20–00:55 Chapter 3: The murmur of the bass

The bass decides where a chord sits

Foundation

Your ears use the bass first to work out "which chord is this". Change the bass and the chord feels different.

Line

When neighbouring chords' bass notes move step by step, they sound like a melody and flow especially smoothly.

Momentum

When the bass alternates or walks, the accompaniment gets legs, and the strum has a groove without needing to be busy.

BuildSlash chords

The five most-used slash chords

How to press them

Hold the usual chord first, then add the bass note after the slash. C/G mostly uses the ring and little fingers; for D/F#, hook the thumb over the neck onto string 6 fret 2.

Strum range

Start the strum from the string the bass note is on: C/E, Am/G and C/G use all six, G/B starts from string 5.

BuildGoing up works too

Ascending bass: C → C/E → F → G

  • A descending bass sounds wistful and nostalgic; an ascending bass feels like climbing and building energy
  • Pre-choruses often use an ascending bass to push into the chorus
  • Use the full barre for F here; string 6 fret 1 is the F bass

Ears follow notes that are "close together" easily. When the bass moves only a fret or two at a time, it sounds like someone singing a melody.

Turn01:05–01:40 Passing notes

Walk the bass from one chord to the next

On the last beat

Passing notes come on the last beat or half beat before a chord change, leading the ear to the next chord.

Use notes from the scale

G to Em: G → F# → E, moving fret by fret through notes in G major.

Use them sparingly

Once or twice every four bars is plenty; too many get in the way of the singer's melody.

Close01:40–02:00 Thinking about bass

Myth or fact?

A slash chord is two chords played together
MythA slash chord is still one chord; it just names the lowest note.
The bass must be the chord's root
MythThe root is the most stable, but thirds, fifths and passing notes can all go in the bass.
C/G and C have the same notes
FactBoth are C, E and G; only the lowest note differs, and the feel changes with it.
Only bass players need to understand bass lines
MythWhen you play and sing, you're the whole band, and a bass line makes one guitar sound fuller.
Descending bass suits ballads well
FactA bass stepping down brings out nostalgia and wistfulness most easily.
The more passing notes, the better
MythThe accompaniment's job is to set off the voice; few and well placed is best.
