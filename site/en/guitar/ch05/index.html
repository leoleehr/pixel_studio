  • 3/4: strong, weak, weak, like turning in a dance
  • 6/8: two big beats, each split in three, like gentle rocking
Build00:20–00:55 Duple and triple time

The two big families of time

2/4

Duple: strong, weak

4/4

Duple: strong, weak, medium, weak

3/4

Triple: strong, weak, weak

6/8

Compound: two big beats, each in three

Simple time

Each beat splits in two: 2/4, 3/4, 4/4. Count "1 & 2 &".

Compound time

Each big beat splits in three: 6/8, 12/8. Count "1 & a 2 & a"; it has a swing to it.

Rule of thumb

Whatever the chord is called, that's the bass note. C plays C, G plays G, Am plays A.

Alternating bass

Move the second bass note to the next string (another note in the chord) and the accompaniment starts to walk.

BuildBass accompaniment in 4/4

Boom, chick-a, boom, chick-a

  • X: the pick plays only the bass string (boom)
  • ↓↑: strum only the treble strings 3, 2 and 1 (chick, a)
  • The small x on beat 3 is the alternating bass, moved to the next string
  • Play only the "boom" first to find the bass, then add "chick-a"

BuildPart 4: Accompaniment in triple time

3/4 waltz: boom, chick, chick

  • Bass on beat 1, treble strums on beats 2 and 3
  • Beat 1 is the heaviest, like the first step in a dance
  • Once it's comfortable, switch to "boom, chick-a, chick-a" and triple time starts to flow
  • Choose the chord set "C G G C": that's the first four bars of Happy Birthday

BuildCompound time

6/8: two big beats, each split in three

  • Count "1 & a 2 & a", with the strong beats on 1 and 2
  • Like 3/4 it has 6 eighth notes, but 3/4 has 3 beats while 6/8 has only 2
  • Accompaniment: boom-chick-a, boom-chick-a, with the second boom on the alternating bass
  • Slow 6/8 is gentle; fast 6/8 sounds like an Irish jig
BuildEasy to mix up

How 3/4 and 6/8 differ

3/4 waltz

Three quarter notes: 1 & 2 & 3 &
Each beat splits in two, and it feels like "one, two, three".

6/8 swing

Two dotted quarter notes: 1 & a 2 & a
Each beat splits in three, and it feels like "one, two" rocking.

Nod along to the music: three nods per bar is 3/4; two nods, each "dragging three little beats", is 6/8.

Turn01:05–01:40 A slow song's best friend

12/8: three little beats inside every beat

  • 4 big beats per bar, each in three, 12 boxes in all
  • Common in old-style love songs, blues and soft rock
  • The Moon River-style slow songs in the original handbook have exactly this sway
  • Slow down to 50 BPM so every stroke sounds clearly

InteractiveMyth busting · flip cards
TurnThinking about time

Myth or fact?

Songs in triple time are always slower
MythTime signature and tempo aren't linked. Fast waltzes are common too.
3/4 and 6/8 are just written differently
MythThey have different numbers of strong beats: 3/4 has three, 6/8 only two big beats.
Playing the bass on the wrong string sounds odd
FactThe bass decides whether a chord sounds settled. Play the E on string 6 under a C chord and the whole chord turns unsettled.
Boom-chick-a only suits slow songs
MythFast country songs often use alternating bass too, and it really drives at speed.
You can play the bass with the thumb or the pick
FactUse the pick when strumming and the thumb when fingerpicking; Chapter 7 starts using the thumb.
You haven't learned 6/8 until you count to 6
MythOnce it's comfortable, just feel the two big beats; counting to 6 actually makes it choppy.
