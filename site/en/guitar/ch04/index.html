Chords set the colour, strumming sets the groove. On the same chords, quarter notes sound like a slow song, 8 beats like folk, and switching to syncopation instantly sounds upbeat.
Build00:20–00:55 Getting the right hand ready

Strum from the wrist and keep the arm steady

  • Pick angle: its centre line points the same way as your index finger and thumb, with 2–3 mm of tip showing
  • Wrist like shaking off water: the elbow stays fixed as the pivot, and the relaxed wrist swings up and down
  • Strum range: downstrokes go from the lowest string you need to string 1; upstrokes hit only the top 3 or 4 treble strings
  • Touch: tilt the pick slightly so it brushes the strings; the sound is rounder and the pick is less likely to catch or drop
BuildPart 1: Guitar strum patterns

Three symbols for writing strums

SymbolNameMotionWhere it usually falls
↓DownstrokeStrum from the thick strings toward the thin onesOn the beat: 1, 2, 3, 4
↑UpstrokeFrom the thin strings toward the thick ones, treble strings onlyHalf beat: &
↓Ghost strumThe hand still swings but stays off the stringsThe stroke that's left out

Remember one rule: the hand never stops swinging. Down, up, down, up, like a clock's pendulum; whether it touches the strings is what changes the rhythm.

BuildPart 2: Strum marks used in Chinese-language books

Learn these three marks before reading Chinese guitar music

MarkSaid asMeaning
XBoomPluck down on the bass strings, for example strings 5, 4 and 3 for a C chord
↓ChickStrum down on the treble strings, for example strings 3, 2 and 1
↑AStrum up on the treble strings, strings 1, 2 and 3

Watch out for reversed arrows

Some Chinese-language books draw arrows following "the strings on the page", the opposite of Western books. When you get new music, check the key first.

How this course writes it

Arrows always show the hand's direction: ↓ toward the floor, ↑ toward the ceiling. Chapter 5's "boom-chick-chick" uses X.

Hide a beat: a tie moves the accent forward

8 beats: eight even strokes

Syncopated folk rock: beat 3 is taken by a tie

The sound on the second half of beat 2 is tied into beat 3. Beat 3 isn't strummed, so the usual "strong, weak, medium, weak" is broken up and it sounds lighter and more driving.

TurnCleaner rhythm

Three small tricks for great-sounding strumming

Left-hand muting

Rest your left-hand fingers lightly on the strings and stay relaxed; strumming gives a percussive "chk chk", perfect for practising the right hand on its own.

Accent on the beat

Downstrokes a bit heavier, upstrokes a bit lighter. When strumming syncopation, make the upstroke in "down-up" clear and the syncopated feel appears.

Strum only the strings you need

C and Am start from string 5, F from string 4; only G uses all six. A clean bass makes a clear chord.

An upstroke starts from string 1, so the hand naturally touches only the first three or four strings. Brushing the treble lightly works like a drummer's hi-hat, setting off the heavy downbeats.

TurnNext level: sixteenth notes

16 beats: each beat splits into four, and your hand swings twice as fast

Counting: 1 e & a

Say "1 e & a" on each beat. "1" and "&" go down, "e" and "a" go up, following the same rule as 8 beats.

Keep the downbeats

The 2013 lesson plan's rule: keep downstrokes on the beat and add variety on the offbeats. The groove is still "strong, weak, medium, weak", with finer detail and more drive.

Myth or fact?

The harder you strum, the better it sounds
MythStrumming too hard makes the pick catch or drop and slows the beat. A relaxed wrist gives a crisp sound.
On a ghost strum, your hand can stop and rest
MythIf your hand stops, the next stroke will be late. A ghost strum means "keep swinging, stay off the strings".
Upstrokes should hit all six strings
MythUpstrokes only need to brush the top 3 or 4 treble strings.
Strumming comes mainly from the wrist
FactThe elbow is the pivot and the wrist does the swinging; big arm swings tire you out quickly.
Syncopation means leaving out one stroke
FactThe stroke that's left out is on beat 3, so the accent moves forward and the groove changes with it.
A metronome gets in the way of practice
MythIf it feels like it's in the way, your beat isn't steady yet. Slow down first, then catch up with the metronome.
