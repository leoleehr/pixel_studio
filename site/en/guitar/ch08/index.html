Why learn G when you already know C?

Choose the most important reason

  1. G is harder, so it's more impressive
  2. There are fewer songs in C
  3. In G you can skip the capo
  4. Everyone's range is different, and lots of songs are in G to begin with
Songs have to fit the singer. G is one of the guitar's most comfortable keys: G, C, D and Em are all open chords that let the strings ring, for a full sound.

Build00:20–00:55 Part 2: The G major scale

Whole-whole-half-whole-whole-whole-half, starting on G

G A B C D E F# G

To keep "whole-whole-half-whole-whole-whole-half", the seventh note has to go up a semitone and become F#.

One sharp

A # on the F line at the start of the music means G major (or E minor).

Green is G

String 6 fret 3, open string 3 and string 1 fret 3: three G's, each an octave apart.

The pattern every major key shares

Major, minor, minor, major, major, minor, diminished. Remember this order and you can lay out the diatonic chords of any key yourself.

Moving to a new key

C → G → Am → F (I–V–vi–IV) in C becomes G → D → Em → C in G.

D: three fingers in a small triangle

  • Index string 3 fret 2, middle string 1 fret 2, ring finger string 2 fret 3
  • Strum from open string 4, skipping strings 6 and 5
  • Going from G to D, the ring finger can act as a "guide", sliding across strings 1 and 2

Barre chords: your index finger is a capo

E

Am
Bm

E shape → F

Move the whole E chord up one fret, and the open strings have to go up too, so the index finger presses all of fret 1 as a new nut.

Am shape → Bm

Move the Am chord up two frets; the index finger barres strings 5 to 1 at fret 2, skipping string 6.

TurnKeys to pressing barre chords

To make it ring, position matters more than strength

1

Side of the index finger

Press with the side of the index finger nearer the thumb, where the bone is firmer.

2

Right against the fret

Keep the index finger snug against the fret; it takes the least effort.

3

Pull back with the arm

Use the weight of your arm to pull your hand toward your body, keeping the web of your thumb relaxed.

4

Thumb low

Put the thumb on the back of the neck, a little below centre, roughly behind the middle finger.

5

String-by-string check

Pluck string by string to find the muted ones.

Strings that often get muted

String 3 gets muted most, because it lands in the crease of the index finger's joint; nudge the index finger up or down a little.

Press, then release

Press firmly for 5 seconds, relax for 5 seconds, ten times. Finger strength builds up gradually.

If it won't ring yet

Use small F, or play Bm on strings 4 to 1 only, and keep the song going.

TurnThinking about barre chords

Myth or fact?

Barre chords don't ring because your fingers aren't strong enough
MythIt's mostly about position and angle. In the right spot, a light press makes them ring.
Barre chords can move around the fretboard
FactMove the F shape up 2 frets and it's G, another place to play a G chord.
High action makes barre chords harder
FactHave a technician lower the action and barre chords get much easier.
For barre chords, the thumb should hook over the top
MythFor barre chords the thumb goes on the back of the neck; hooking it over the top stops the index finger lying flat.
If your hand aches after barre chords, stop and rest
FactRest when it aches and practise again tomorrow. A little every day and you'll feel the difference in two or three weeks.
Learn barre chords and you can play most chords
FactThe E-shape and Am-shape barres, moved around, give you every major and minor chord.
