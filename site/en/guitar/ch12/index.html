The chorus's high note is out of your range. What's the best fix?

Choose the best solution

  1. Shout your way up
  2. Switch to another song
  3. Move the whole song to a lower key
  4. Skip the high line
The original key was tailored to the original singer. Lower the whole song together and the relationship between melody and chords stays the same; it just fits your voice better. That's "transposing".

Build00:20–00:55 Chapter 6: Changing key and transposing

Transposing: every chord moves the same distance

Original key CCGAmFEmDm
Up 2 semitones → DDABmGF#mEm
Down 3 semitones → AAEF#mDC#mBm
Seen as scale degreesIVviIViiiii

Two ways

① Change the chords: move every chord by the same number of semitones. ② Use a capo: keep the shapes, and each fret higher raises it a semitone.

Scale degrees are the most useful

Remember the song is I–V–vi–IV and you can lay out the chords in any key straight away.

Transposing practice

Move G → D → Em → C into C (down 7 semitones or up 5). What are the chords?

  1. C → F → Dm → Bb
  2. C → G → Am → F
  3. C → G → Em → F
  4. A → E → F#m → D
I–V–vi–IV in G is still I–V–vi–IV in C: C → G → Am → F.

The song is in E and you want to use C shapes. Which fret does the capo go on?

  1. Fret 4
  2. Fret 3
  3. Fret 5
  4. No capo needed
C → C# → D → D# → E is four semitones, so fret 4. You could also use G shapes at fret 9, but that's very high up.
Build2. How to choose a song's key: Make Your Own Key

Four steps to work out your key

1

Find the song's highest note

Usually in the chorus; check against the original or the music.

2

Measure your own highest note

The highest note you can sing easily without straining, not your absolute limit.

3

Work out the gap

However many semitones apart the two notes are, move the whole song by that many.

4

Choose shapes and capo

Pick the open chord shapes you're most comfortable with, then make up the difference with a capo.

Turn01:05–01:40 Chapter 8: An intro to songwriting

Writing a song starts with a story

Theme and story

Characters, events, time, place, objects: who the main character is, what happens, when, where, what's left behind. The more specific, the more moving.

Point of view

Tell your own story as "I", or someone else's as "he" or "she". Point of view decides where the listener stands.

Rhyme

End lines on words that rhyme (for example the -ou rhyme: 行走, 雙手, 長久), and lyrics flow and stick. You can keep one rhyme throughout or change rhyme between sections.

Song form

The verse tells the story and the chorus makes the point. The song-form map from Chapter 6 works as a framework for writing too.

Capture it

Ideas come and go fast, so record them on your phone as voice memos or notes whenever they come.

Revise and get feedback

Leave it for a day after writing, then sing it to a friend and revise line by line. Most good songs are rewritten again and again.

TurnRhyme practice

Find the set whose line endings share one rhyme

Choose the set that rhymes all the way through

  1. 天空, 海洋, 星星 (sky, sea, stars)
  2. 放手, 以後, 溫柔 (let go, later, gentle)
  3. 思念, 遠方, 季節 (longing, far away, season)
  4. 回憶, 離開, 雨天 (memory, leave, rainy day)
放手, 以後 and 溫柔 all end on the -ou rhyme. You can rhyme every line, or only the ends of sections; the latter sounds most natural.

Common rhymes

-a rhyme: 牽掛, 說話, 回家, 櫻花
-an rhyme: 藍天, 身邊, 遙遠, 思念
-ang rhyme: 月光, 遠方, 歌唱, 夢想
-ou rhyme: 行走, 雙手, 長久, 以後
-i rhyme: 想你, 早起, 真心, 回憶

CloseReference: Chapter 12, caring for an acoustic guitar

Help your guitar stay with you for years

Body partHow to care for it
FretboardWipe it clean when changing strings; oil rosewood and ebony fretboards once or twice a year
BodyWipe off sweat with a dry cloth after playing; keep it out of sun, heat and damp
StringsWipe the strings after playing; change them when they sound dull or show rust, roughly every one to two months if you play often
EnvironmentHumidity around 45–55%; in Taiwan's summer use desiccant packs or a dry cabinet

Types of folk guitar strings

Phosphor bronze: warm and durable, the most common. 80/20 bronze: bright, but dulls faster. Coated strings: smooth feel and longer life, at a higher price.

How to change strings

Change one string at a time → fix the ball end with the bridge pin → leave two or three frets' length to wind round the peg → wind in one direction → tune, gently stretch the string, and tune again.
