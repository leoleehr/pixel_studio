The same two notes, plucked versus hammered

The teacher plucks two notes, then plucks one and hammers the next. What difference do you hear?

  1. Different pitch
  2. The second is more connected and smoother
  3. The second is louder
  4. They're exactly the same
With a hammer-on, the second note isn't plucked by the right hand; the left hand alone makes it sound, so the two notes join up, like legato in singing.

The original handbook's Chapter 5

If you play melodies on guitar only the ordinary way, songs sound flat and lack expression. Hammer-ons, pull-offs and slides are the three basic moves that make a melody speak.

Three basic moves: the right hand plucks only once

TechniqueWritten asMotionSound
Hammer-onHAfter plucking the lower note, a left-hand finger strikes a higher fret like a hammerConnects upward
Pull-offPAfter plucking the higher note, a left-hand finger flicks down off the stringConnects downward
SlideS、／、＼Keep pressure on the string and slide along it to another fretLike a vocal glide

Hammer-ons need to be quick

Drop the finger quickly from above and strike with the fingertip right by the fret.

Pull-offs go toward the floor

Flick the finger toward the floor, as if plucking lightly.

Slides keep the pressure on

Keep the pressure on throughout so the sound stays connected.

Turn01:05–01:40 Chapter 8: Muting, chucks, slaps, harmonics

Imitating a drum kit on guitar

TechniqueHow to do itSoundWhere it's used
Palm muteRest the side of your right palm lightly on the strings by the saddle, then pick or strumShort, thick, crispVerses, rock bass parts
ChuckAs you strum down, your right palm lands on the strings and cuts the sound"Chk", like a snareBeats 2 and 4
SlapHit the strings or body with the thumb or side of the palmPercussiveFingerstyle, funk
HarmonicTouch the left hand lightly right above fret 12, 7 or 5, keep it light, and let go straight after pluckingClear and bell-likeIntros, outros

The original handbook's Chapters 8 and 9 only list technique names; this chapter adds the motions and practice from class.

TurnChuck

Down-up, chk-up, down-up, chk-up

  • Where the ✕ is: as you strum down, the side of your right palm presses onto the strings
  • Relax the left hand a little at the same time so the chord is cut off too
  • Practise the "chk" on muted strings first, then add the chord
  • Swap beats 2 and 4 of 8 beats for chucks and you've got a drum feel

TurnNatural harmonics

Frets 12, 7 and 5: a light touch brings out a bell

Where

Put your finger right above the fret wire, not in between two frets.

Touch

Only just touch the string and keep it light; let go straight after plucking.

Pitch

The fret-12 harmonic is an octave above the open string; fret 7 is a fifth higher again; fret 5 is two octaves up.

Give long notes more expression

TechniqueHow to do it
Bend ↗︎Hold the string and push it up so the pitch rises. Acoustic strings are thicker, so even a semitone bend has real impact
ReleaseAfter bending up, let it slowly back down
Vibrato ~Press the string and rock the finger slightly side to side or up and down so the pitch wavers a little
TrillRapid hammer-ons and pull-offs between two notes, like birdsong

Close01:40–02:00 Thinking about technique

Myth or fact?

The more techniques, the better the song
MythTechniques are like seasoning; they only sound good in the right places.
Hammer-ons being quieter is normal
FactOnly the left hand makes the sound, so it's naturally a little quieter; getting the two notes close in volume is a great result.
You can bend on an acoustic guitar too
FactYou can, but the strings are thicker so the range is limited. A semitone bend is most common.
Harmonics need a firm press
MythOnly touch lightly. Press down and it becomes an ordinary note.
Chucks have to be loud
MythA chuck is like a light snare hit; too hard and it drowns out the voice.
Vibrato gives long notes more feeling
FactLike a singer's vibrato on a long note, it adds expression.
