OpenA word from Leo

The hardest part of writing a song

Writing a song isn't hard. What's hard is building sharp powers of observation in daily life,
and training yourself to record things faster and more efficiently.
Hardest of all is carrying that feeling, that moment of being moved,
across whole into the listener's ears, and even their heart.

〈月亮代表我的心〉 and 〈綠島小夜曲〉 are still sung today for a simple reason: every listener finds themselves in the song.

OpenWhat writing means

Input, then output

News, conversations, travel, a lovely melody: all of these are inputs. Once information enters your mind and gets sorted, out come the words and melodies we want.

InputWhat you see and feel
InterpretWhat it means
AnalyseKeywords and emotions
SortPick usable material
ArrangeOpen, build, turn, close
OutputLyrics and melody

Word sources and hooks

The keywords of an article are enough to build a "word source"; its main point carries enough weight to hold up a strong hook. Together they form a song's source and its signature.

The safest way to start

Imitate the songwriters you love, find your direction in their work, then develop a style of your own. A melody someone has used before, once reworked, can become another song.

Art isn't a black-and-white science. Once an idea turns into action, nobody can easily overturn the work that comes out. Good music doesn't need a pile of advanced technique; using familiar ideas and skills to fully express what you feel is the art nobody can copy.

Over time, art and commerce swap places

Today's art, tomorrow's commerce

Kuo Ying-nan's Amis songs, ChthoniC's Taiwanese folk elements: art that once belonged to a niche later reached the world stage and became sounds everyone knows.

Today's commerce, tomorrow's art

Michael Jackson, Teresa Teng: the hits of their day, sung for decades, became the cultural memory of an era.

〈老人飲酒歌〉, sung by Kuo Ying-nan and the Malan Chorus, was sampled by the German group Enigma and reworked as "Return To Innocence". It aired in promotional videos for the 1996 Atlanta Olympics, and the whole world heard the voices of Taiwan's Indigenous people.

BuildIdeas about writing

Myth or fact?

Pop songs have no rules; just write whatever comes
MythPop songs have set forms and formulas. Grasp the big principles and your writing has direction.
People who don't play an instrument can't write good songs
MythHumming, recording and writing words down are all songwriting. An instrument helps, but it's no entry requirement.
You have to have lyrics before you can write music
MythThere's no rule about words or music first; writing the melody and then the lyrics is very common.
All art begins with imitation
FactAnalyse the work you love, imitate its techniques, then develop a style of your own.
You can copy someone else's lyrics word for word
MythYou imitate the techniques and structure; copying word for word is infringement, and it loses the point of writing.
Pop music reflects its era, and it's an art of conversation
FactA song talks to its listeners and records what its era looked like.

BuildDeciding the overall style

Once the theme is settled, decide what the song sounds like

M

Melody

Traditional scales, pentatonic scales, the blues scale. The pentatonic scale has an Eastern colour; the blues scale has laziness and tension.

H

Harmony

Harmony and chord writing. The same melody with a different set of chords changes mood at once.

R

Rhythm

How the groove is shared out, rhythmic patterns and styles: ballad, rock, R&B, hip-hop, folk.

You don't have to settle the style all at once. Write down a direction first, such as "a mid-slow, piano-led ballad", and refine it when you arrange in Lesson 7.

BuildSources of material

Words first or music first: there's no rule

MelodyHarmonyRhythm
The three elements of music

The fourth element: lyrics

On top of the three elements, a song has lyrics that set the scene. The melody handles the emotion; the lyrics handle the pictures and the story.

  • Words first: write music following the sections and sounds of the text
  • Music first: write words following the melody's contour and note lengths
  • Both together: hum and write at the same time, each fitting the other
BuildFour terms that are easy to mix up

Writing vs. setting lyrics, composing vs. setting music

TermHow it's doneWhat to watch for
Writing lyricsWriting lyrics directly, following a pop song's formThe words come first, so groove and sound need thought when the music is written
Setting lyricsFitting lyrics to a finished melodyThe melody's highs and lows and the words' tones decide whether it's easy to sing
ComposingWriting a melody directlyFind a motif first, then develop it into a full phrase
Setting musicWriting a melody for finished lyricsThe melody has to fit the lines' sections, lengths and emotions

The teacher wrote both words and music for 〈El Nino〉 and 〈愛情的彼端〉; for 〈忘了〉, the lyrics were written by asreal, a user on PTT's Create board, and the teacher set them to music.

Turn01:30–02:30 Gathering everyday material

Build your own banks of words, lines and melodies

  • Words: a word you like, such as candlelight, far away, goodnight
  • Lines: a line that paints a picture, such as "the rain stopped at the second crossing"
  • Melodies: a melody you suddenly hum, written down in numbered notation or recorded
  • Index them by theme or rhyme so you can find them the moment you need them

Take three minutes now and add at least two items to each bank. The material is saved on this device and works across all eight lessons.

TurnChoosing material

Good or bad material comes down to whether it fits

Better material

Like musical tones: pleasing and orderly. Ornate or plain wording both work, like the words of Lin Xi, Yao Chien and Vincent Fang.

Weaker material

Like noise: extra filler and lines that don't fit the scene distract the listener.

Material that fits

It fits the logic, the scene, the rhetoric and the delivery. Casual, impressionistic or self-healing work has its place too.

"At the bus stop at dawn, we wait side by side; stocks fell three points today; your scarf still holds last night's warmth."
The second line has nothing to do with the scene; it's filler. Cut it, and the picture comes into focus at once.

TurnThe hook

The hook: the part you can't forget after one listen

Lyric hook

A-Mei's 〈我要快樂〉 and David Tao's 〈就是愛你〉: the title is the chorus's first line, repeated again and again, so listeners naturally hum along.

Musical hook

Pachelbel's Canon: one bass line and chord set keeps looping while the melody above changes layer by layer.

A&R：Artist & Repertory

The role at a record label that plans a singer's image and songs, deciding what kind of songs an album needs. Lyricists such as Wawa and Liao Ying-ru have both been A&R directors.

Writing the song is only step two

To get from an idea into someone's headphones, a song still goes through arranging, recording and mixing. Lesson 7 covers the later stages in full.

Close02:30–03:00 The writer's mindset

Three taboos and three goals of writing

Three taboos

  1. Wanting your first song to be a classic
  2. Pushing on and on, forgetting the good melody you hummed earlier
  3. Writing a section only when inspired, in fits and starts, with no structure

Three goals

  1. Finish the song
  2. Hum the melody clearly
  3. Always record it

Finished beats perfect. The job of your first song is to make you believe you can finish one.
