Note names, solfège and numbered notation

Note namesCDEFGABC
SolfègeDoReMiFaSolLaSiDo
Numbered notation12345671̇
DistanceWWHWWWH

Mi to Fa and Ti to Do are a half step apart

All other neighbouring notes are a whole step apart. The major scale's pattern is "whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half".

Highs and lows in numbered notation

A dot above a number means an octave up; a dot below means an octave down. This course always writes melodies in numbered notation.

Time signature

4/4: a quarter note gets one beat, four beats to a bar. 2/4: a quarter note gets one beat, two beats to a bar. The bottom number says which note gets one beat; the top number says how many beats are in a bar.

Dot

A dot after a note adds half its length again. A dotted quarter note is 1.5 beats.

Designing the groove gives a melody more depth. Put important words on strong beats and filler words on weak ones; move the accent in the same lyric line and the emotion changes at once. The groove design chart in the original materials is practice in reassigning strong and weak across eight beats.

BuildWhat pop music is

Pop music: simplify complicated theory so music belongs to everyone

1

Roots

Pop music began with the blues; over a hundred years it blended every kind of style and turned the whole world's music upside down.

2

Taiwan

While US forces were stationed in Taiwan in the 1950s, Taiwan took in a great deal of American pop culture and later became a centre of Mandarin pop.

3

Character

It simplifies the complicated theory and structure of classical music so music is approachable, while keeping set patterns and techniques.

What songwriting is: following the patterns of pop music to join words and melody and tell a story people can sing. Once you grasp the big principles, anyone can write pop music.

BuildWays to develop a melody

Motif, repetition, sequence, variation, linking

01

Motif

A short, distinctive figure, the seed of a melody

02

Repetition

The same motif comes back to make it stick

03

Sequence

Same shape, moved up or down as a whole

04

Variation

Inversion, retrograde, augmentation and diminution change how the motif looks

05

Linking

String several motifs together and land back on the tonic to finish a phrase

A song's melody is rarely new notes from start to finish. In the chorus of 〈十年〉, "十年之前" and "十年之後" use the same motif with only the words changed: that's the power of repetition.

How a song opens: intro and verse

Intro前奏

Function
Leads into the verse, announces the song, and sets the mood for singer and listener
Character
Often the instrumental hook, with strong melody and rhythm; usually four to eight bars, often a slightly changed chorus or verse melody
Order
Nothing comes before the intro, and the verse always follows it

Verse主歌

Words
Tells the story; the lyrics are busier and the plot calmer, warming the song up
Music
The tune is plainer, low and level; usually two verses that set the key and rhyme, with a slight change at the end of the second
Order
Can lead to the pre-chorus or chorus, and can be reached from the intro or interlude
TurnRaising and releasing energy

The pre-chorus builds the heat; the chorus releases it

Pre-chorus前副歌

Words
Adds the feelings the verse hasn't finished saying; it's where the plot turns. Its syllable count and metre clearly differ from the verse, and it can change rhyme
Music
The tune starts to rise, the groove is livelier than the verse, and it can use more chords
Order
Sits between verse and chorus and always leads to the chorus; it can be left out when the verse already has enough momentum

Chorus副歌

Words
The most heated moment; easy to remember and hum, with clear, crisp rhymes, usually the same rhyme across both choruses
Music
The song's selling point, repeated to build memory; the tune is high with big swings, and the instruments' energy and density peak
Order
The first chorus is often followed by an interlude, the second by a bridge, then back to the chorus
TurnBuffers and turns

Interlude and post-chorus (bridge)

Interlude間奏

Function
A buffer between two big sections that strengthens the instrumental hook
Character
Often an instrumental solo of four to eight bars; often changes key for a turn, then returns to the original key
Order
Follows a chorus, and can lead back to the verse or pre-chorus

Post-chorus / bridge後副歌／橋段

Words
A more forceful addition after the chorus: the answer, the letting go, the hardest moment to part; it can be left out when the chorus is already complete
Music
A different flavour from the verse and chorus; the tempo can go to half or double time to make the groove's rise and fall stronger
Order
Follows a chorus or interlude, and always leads back to the chorus
TurnHow a song ends

The outro lets the emotion out; the ending leaves an afterglow

Outro尾奏

Function
Lets the emotion out before the song ends, so it doesn't freeze all of a sudden
Character
The voice finishes and the instruments gradually wind down; usually four or eight bars, ideally stopping on the dominant to prepare the ending
Order
Follows the second or third chorus, and always leads to the ending
Ways to endEffect
Full stopAll instruments stop together, ending on a rest
RitardandoAll instruments gradually slow down into the tonic chord
Fade outThe overall volume drops gradually to silence
ImprovisedThe instruments keep improvising, then stop together
Unison figureA designed ending phrase, grand and ceremonial
Sudden stopEvery sound stops at once for a special effect

TurnSettling motive and intent

Before you write, settle the song's subject

Five settings

  • Lyrical theme: what the song wants to say
  • Genre: ballad, rock, R&B, folk
  • Tempo: slow, moderate, fast
  • Singer: male, female or a group
  • Listener: who the song speaks to

The song's style and type

Slow ballads usually sit at 60 to 80 BPM, mid-tempo pop at 80 to 110 BPM, and upbeat dance tracks often above 110 BPM. Once the tempo is set, the verse's syllable count and the chorus's length have something to go on.

A love theme about the feelings after a breakup and letting go; a slow ballad in 4/4, marked in the original materials at 63 beats per minute; told in the first person, with the piano intro as a hook along with the contrast of "十年之前／十年之後".
