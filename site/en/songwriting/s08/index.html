Four steps once a song is finished

01

Listen again and again

Self-reflection: listen again the next day and find what doesn't flow

02

Analyse objectively

Audience reception: ask different people to listen and gather their views

03

Revise again

Balance both: keep your original intent and take on sensible suggestions

04

Keep the cycle going

Build00:25–01:20 How the workshop works

Giving feedback: one compliment, one question, one suggestion

＋

Compliment

Name the lyric line or melody that moved you most, and why.

？

Question

What wasn't clear as you listened: the point of view, the plot, the meaning of a line.

→

Suggestion

A direction they can act on right away, such as repeating the chorus once or changing a word's tone.

Feedback is about the song, not the person. Say "this line's rhyme feels a bit forced", not "you're bad at rhyming".

BuildLearning to weigh others' criticism

When you get feedback, first work out where it's coming from

Expert advice
Consider it seriouslyIt points out specific problems and reasons, and is usually worth taking on.
Amateur impressions
Just as importantMost listeners are amateurs, and their impressions are closest to how the market will react.
Malicious criticism
Don't take it to heartCriticism with no reasons, only emotion, can't help your song.
Comments born of jealousy
Stay level-headedIf your song gets under someone's skin, it has power.
Positive encouragement
Take it gladlyNote what people like; your style may be hiding there.
When to rewrite
Rule of thumbWhen three or more people point out the same problem, it's time to rewrite.

BuildWorkshop example

Practise today's feedback method on a classic first

  • Compliment: how the chorus line 「曲終人散的寂寞」 is both the title and the hook
  • Question: the verse is set at a wedding; what does the metonymy 「一身紅」 ("all in red") refer to?
  • Suggestion: if you arranged it, which instrument would you add in the chorus?

Shih Yi-lang sets the bustle of a wedding against the loneliness of loss, keeps one rhyme throughout, and builds the chorus step by step; Phil Chang's melody lifts the range in the chorus, releasing the pent-up emotion all at once. The scene, point of view and rhymes stay consistent throughout: a model of a well-built song.

True or false: ideas from the eight lessons

When setting lyrics you don't need to think about singability; that's the singer's job
FalseSet lyrics to follow the melody's contour and the tones, to reduce clashes between words and music.
Setting music means composing to the content and sections of the lyrics
TrueWords first, then music: that's setting music.
Analysing lyricists' techniques and rhetoric is an important reference for your own lyrics
TrueAll art begins with imitation, and what you imitate is technique.
A song is like a story and needs an opening, development, turn and close
TrueThe verse tells the story, the pre-chorus builds the heat, the chorus releases it, and the outro wraps up.
Express what you really think; a bit negative or extreme is fine
FalseIdeas that are too negative or extreme are one of the nine taboos of lyrics.
Pop music reflects its era, and it's an art of conversation
TrueA song talks to its listeners and records its era too.
CloseConclusion

The essence of songwriting, sharing your work and self-fulfilment

Essence

Record the details of life in melody and words, and carry what you feel and what moves you across whole into the listener's heart.

Sharing

After a song is released, you can share it with friends, post it online or enter contests, so more people can hear it.

Self-fulfilment

Good music doesn't need a pile of advanced technique. Fully expressing what you feel is an art nobody can copy.

The road of songwriting is long, and we'll always need each other's encouragement. Eight lessons are only the start; your next song begins with an idea tonight.
