OpenRhetorical techniques

Twenty-three figures of speech in five families

Expressing feeling

Exclamation, rhetorical question, apostrophe

Transforming images

Metaphor, transference, symbolism, metonymy, hyperbole, sensory description

Contrast and rhythm

Contrast, antithesis, parallelism

Repetition and chaining

Repetition, climax, anadiplosis, palindrome

Wordplay

Allusion, parody, pun, irony, two-part allegorical saying, inversion, alliteration and assonance

How to use them

Rhetoric brings words to life, but cram too many techniques into one line and listeners can't find the point.

Build00:25–01:20 Expressing feeling

Exclamation, rhetorical question, apostrophe

Exclamation

Uses interjections and particles to show surprise, sorrow, regret or hope.

怎麼了 你累了 說好的幸福呢Vincent Fang 〈說好的幸福呢〉

Rhetorical question

Turns suddenly from statement to question to catch attention and make the other person reflect.

你還記得嗎 記憶裡的炎夏Lee Cheuk-hung 〈愛〉

Apostrophe

Calls out and talks directly to someone who isn't there, strengthening first-person feeling.

分手快樂 祝你快樂 你可以找到更好的Yao Jo-lung 〈分手快樂〉
BuildTransforming images (1)

Metaphor, transference, symbolism

Metaphor

Uses one thing to describe another: the known to explain the unknown, the concrete to explain the abstract.

愛情好像流沙 我不掙扎Wawa 〈流沙〉

Transference

Changes a thing's nature: objects as people, people as objects, the abstract made concrete.

愛情不停站 想開往地老天荒Lin Xi 〈愛情轉移〉

Symbolism

Uses concrete images to express unseen ideas and feelings.

沒結果的花 未完成的牽掛Lee Cheuk-hung 〈愛〉
BuildTransforming images (2)

Metonymy, hyperbole, sensory description

Metonymy

Doesn't use the thing's real name, but something related in its place.

你最後一身紅 殘留在我眼中Shih Yi-lang 〈曲終人散〉

Hyperbole

Goes beyond the facts to deepen the impression; coming from real feeling, it won't be mistaken for fact.

我的心已經飛到這個城市的另一邊Wawa 〈天天〉

Sensory description

Depicts the sounds, colours and states of things to make the picture three-dimensional.

天青色等煙雨 而我在等妳Vincent Fang 〈青花瓷〉
BuildContrast and rhythm

Contrast, antithesis, parallelism

Contrast

Puts opposite ideas side by side; the stronger the contrast, the sharper the impression.

牽手的時候太冷清 擁抱的時候不夠靠近Yang Li-te 〈他不愛我〉

Antithesis

Two lines with the same number of characters and similar syntax, neat in form and harmonious in sound.

人變了心 言而無信 人斷了情 無謂傷心Lin Chiu-li 〈剪愛〉

Parallelism

Three or more lines in a row presenting images of the same kind; they needn't be the same length.

我不要你的承諾 不要你的永遠Wu Yu-kang 〈原來你什麼都不要〉
BuildRepetition and chaining

Repetition, climax, anadiplosis, palindrome

Repetition

Repeats the same word in a row to show time and space stretching on.

我願意為你 我願意為你Yao Chien 〈我願意〉

Climax

Arranges things layer by layer by depth, order or size.

一步兩步三步四步望著天Jay Chou 〈星晴〉

Anadiplosis

The end of one line becomes the start of the next.

說再見 再見不會太遙遠Ding Hsiao-wen 〈祝福〉

Palindrome

Mostly the same words, in exactly reversed order.

孤單是一個人的狂歡 狂歡是一群人的孤單Chen Hsiao-chuan 〈葉子〉
BuildWordplay (1)

Allusion, parody, pun

Allusion

Borrows famous sayings, classical references or proverbs, using common ground to persuade.

寫下我 度秒如年難捱的離騷Qing Feng 〈小情歌〉

Parody

Swaps words in a familiar phrase to say something new; common in song and album titles.

陽光宅男, 無樂不作, 魔杰座Song and album titles

Pun

Uses a word's multiple meanings or a sound-alike to give a line a double meaning.

你說水星它沒有衛星好孤獨Lin Xi 〈想哭〉
BuildWordplay (2)

Irony, two-part allegorical saying, inversion, alliteration and assonance

Irony

Says the opposite of what's meant to express what's meant; often used for satire.

這個家園充滿芬芳的青草Tom Chang 〈動物的悲歌〉

Two-part allegorical saying

The first half makes a comparison and the second half explains it; also called a hidden saying.

我乾杯你隨意Hsu Chang-te 〈婚禮的祝福〉

Inversion

Deliberately reverses normal word order; common in work with a classical flavour.

如傳世的青花瓷自顧自美麗Vincent Fang 〈青花瓷〉

Alliteration and assonance

Uses words that share initials or finals to strengthen the rhythm.

嘆惜著誰又被傷了心 卻還不清醒Lin Chiu-li 〈聽海〉

Which figure of speech does this line use?

「因為有你，寂寞它就離去」

  1. Rhetorical question
  2. Allusion
  3. Transference
  4. Anadiplosis
"Loneliness" leaves: an abstract feeling is given a human action, which is transference in the form of personification.

「你的愛不能給，能給的是她已在你身邊」

  1. Rhetorical question
  2. Parallelism
  3. Allusion
  4. Anadiplosis
The first clause ends on 「給」 and the next begins with 「能給」: anadiplosis.

BuildQuiz: former students' songs

Two more challenges

「有太多太多看不見的未來，有太多太多看得見的現在」

  1. Rhetorical question
  2. Repetition
  3. Allusion
  4. Anadiplosis
「太多太多」 repeats back to back, which is repetition; the contrast between the two lines also has the effect of contrast.

「只能看著無法透視的悲傷，你們能不能幫助我繼續堅強？」

  1. Rhetorical question
  2. Parallelism
  3. Allusion
  4. Anadiplosis
Turning from statement to question to catch the listener's attention: a rhetorical question.

TurnConclusions and advice

Three directions for writing lyrics

Settle your theme and market direction

Pop music is an art of conversation and a reflection of its era. Find the question, find the material, find the answer: the answer is what you really want to say, then tell the story step by step.

Make good use of rhetoric and tools

Package the title and hook with care; keep a dictionary, a word bank and a line bank sorted by sound; collect words and lines all the time, connect your ideas, and give them a good name.

All art begins with imitation

Understand a song's sections and formats, analyse lyricists' techniques and rhetoric, and read widely across every kind of writing. You may never become Vincent Fang, but you might find a Lin Xi of your own.

TurnAlways be ready to fix your lyrics

Fix-it list

  • Are the language and lines appropriate, logical and building step by step?
  • Will the lines stand the test of time: too trendy, or too clichéd?
  • How singable the words are
  • Is the repeated hook landing?
  • Avoid forced rhymes that sacrifice meaning for the rhyme
  • Avoid wavering ideas, with the person and stance always changing
  • Learn to weigh others' criticism: expert, amateur, malicious, jealous or sincere
  • Judge when to rewrite to make the facts more engaging and more moving
