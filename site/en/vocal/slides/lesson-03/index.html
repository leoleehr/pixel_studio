Build00:20–00:55 1. What resonance is

Resonance is the amplifier of your singing voice

The simplest definition of resonance: an object sounding because it vibrates in sympathy.

The raw sound from the vocal folds is like a single string on its own, faint and thin. Only with the sympathetic vibration of the body's spaces does it become a ringing, sustained singing voice.

After the vote, the teacher demonstrates on guitar: the difference between holding the body and letting it go.

If a guitar had only strings and no body, how would the sound change?

  1. It gets louder
  2. Hardly any change
  3. Faint, thin, quickly gone
  4. The pitch goes up
The body is the guitar's resonating box. The strings decide the pitch, while volume and tone depend on the box amplifying them. The voice's resonating boxes are the mouth, head and chest.

Build2. The three resonating spaces

The vocal tract and where the three resonances are felt

Head resonance: where it is and how to find it
Vibration felt in the head and the bridge of the noseHum /m/ with your mouth closed; the bridge of the nose and between the eyebrows tingle. The tone is soft and rich; be careful it doesn't turn into a squeezed, nasal sound.
Mouth resonance: where it is and how to find it
The shape of the mouth and pharynx shapes the toneWhen you yawn, the soft palate lifts and the back of the mouth opens. The tone is crisp and bright; be careful not to mistake "opening" for squeezing hard.
Chest resonance: where it is and how to find it
A hand on the chest feels the low vibrationPut your palm on your chest and say "mm-hmm" low. The tone is deep and solid; be careful not to press the larynx down and make "fake depth".

Build4. Mouth resonance｜Crisp and bright

Opening the mouth: the key is to "hold it open", not "force it open"

How to do it

Pretend to yawn and remember the "big space" at the back of your mouth: soft palate up, tongue root relaxed, jaw hanging naturally.

Keep that space and sigh gently on "ha".

Common mistakes

Mistaking "opening" the throat for squeezing hard: veins stand out on the neck and the sound gets stiff and stuck.

The /a/ and /o/ vowels in Taiwanese songs open wider, which makes them perfect for practising that open feeling.

Build5. Shifting resonance｜Chest → head

Slide from chest to head and watch how pitch and volume change

  1. Predict first: as the pitch goes up, does the volume get louder or softer?
  2. Press "Start recording" and slide slowly on /m/ or /u/ from a low chest note to a high head note
  3. Watch the two curves: the yellow line is pitch, the blue is volume
  4. Drag across part of the chart to label it chest, mouth or head
  5. Download the chart, or upload it to the teacher

As resonance moves from chest to head, the frequency rises and the volume (amplitude) drops noticeably. That's normal: through the register break, bring the volume down and keep the mouth open so the two sounds hand over gradually, instead of pushing harder.

BuildHead resonance ≠ nasal sound

Nose-pinch test: check whether your sound is stuck in your nose

  • Sing a comfortable long /a/ and record a version "without pinching" first
  • Sing the same note again, and pinch your nose halfway through
  • Listen to both: if the sound gets clearly muffled, air is going through the nose and making it nasal
  • If the sound hardly changes, your resonance is in the right place

Open your mouth a little more, lift the soft palate and imagine sending the sound forward from between your eyebrows rather than squeezing it out of your nostrils.

Build6. Chest resonance｜Deep and solid

Put your palm on your chest and feel the low vibration

How to do it

Three notes below your speaking pitch, say /hei/, or say a low "mm-hmm".

When your palm feels the chest vibrate, that's chest resonance. Like telling someone what's on your mind, no force needed.

Common mistake: fake depth

Pressing the throat down to make a "really deep" sound.

It sounds full, but the throat is tight, high notes become hard to reach and you tire quickly.

Turn01:05–01:40 Find your register break

Siren slides: find where the curve breaks

  • On /u/ or /ng/, slide slowly from your lowest note to your highest, then back again
  • Like an ambulance siren, keep it continuous in the middle
  • Where the sound suddenly breaks and the tone changes is your register break
  • The moment you hear the change, press "Mark break", then use the keyboard to check which note it is

Red dots show possible break points. Jumps in the curve can also come from the microphone, so go by the change you hear yourself.

Turn7. Mixing resonances

All three resonances work together, in different proportions

Without the polish of head resonance the voice sounds dry and hard; without the support of chest resonance it sounds thin and floaty.

Three tips for getting through the register break

① Bring the volume down a little
② Keep the mouth open
③ Let chest and head hand over gradually instead of pushing harder

In the high range, which resonance takes a bigger share?

  1. Chest resonance
  2. Mouth resonance
  3. Head resonance
  4. High notes don't need resonance
The high range uses more head resonance, the low range leans on the chest, and the mouth stays open all the way. Pushing high notes with chest resonance easily cracks the voice or turns into shouting.
