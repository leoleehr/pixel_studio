Sing the same line at 70, 100 and 140 BPM

Long notes

Stretch every word to its full length, with quick in slow out supporting the line ends.

100

Short notes

Separate the words slightly and the groove appears by itself.

Staccato

Build3. Why imitate

Which kind of singer would you most like to imitate?

Unless you were born able to make any melody sound moving, every singer develops through imitation.

Imitation is for developing the techniques you lack; the more varied the singers you imitate, the more techniques you can use.

BuildThree steps of imitation

Listen, imitate, transform

Listen

Listen to the same line five times

Mark the breaths, resonance, runs, breathiness and line endings in turn.

Imitate

Imitate line by line and record

Play it alternately with the original and compare line by line.

Transform

Keep what suits you

Keep the techniques that suit your voice and let go of the rest.

If your throat starts to tighten or hurt, stop at once. It means you're imitating a way of singing that doesn't suit you, especially a husky sound made by deliberately pressing the throat.

BreathsWhere the breaths are, and whether they're full breaths or sneaked ones
ResonanceWhich resonance the verse and chorus each use
RunsWhich words have runs, and how many notes they move through
BreathinessWhere a bit of air is added on purpose
