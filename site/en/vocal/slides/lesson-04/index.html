Tongue twister: fast, but also clear

四是四，十是十，十四是十四，四十是四十。("Four is four, ten is ten, fourteen is fourteen, forty is forty.")

One wrong word and your time doesn't count. Speed is easy; the hard part is keeping the flat-tongue 四 (sì) and the retroflex 十 (shí) both perfectly clear.

Vowels can be stretched and carry the melody, so you can recognise a song from its vowels alone; consonants are short and have almost no pitch. Vowels are the body of singing, and consonants make the lyrics understandable.
Build00:20–00:55 1. Vowels and consonants

Consonants short and clear, vowels long and round

Vowel

Makes it sound good

Long notes almost always land on the vowel of the last word in a line. Keep the vowel round and the resonance can open up.

Consonants

Makes it understood

Consonants come before vowels: make them clear the instant they leave your mouth, then hand straight over to the vowel.

A song is a chain of notes, rhythms and harmonies. Trying to express emotion without a solid vocal foundation is like holding a gun that isn't loaded.

Build2. The five basic vowels

Guess the mouth shape first, then practise once in front of the mirror

[a]　ㄚ
Jaw drops naturallyTongue tip resting lightly against the lower teeth. If the mouth opens wide but the throat doesn't, the sound actually goes flat.
[i]　ㄧ、ㄩ
Corners of the mouth spread slightly, tongue raisedKeep space inside the mouth; too flat or too pointed turns into a squeezed sound.
[u]　ㄨ
Lips rounded and forwardLike a gentle whistle; watch that it doesn't get too quiet or muffled.
[e]　ㄟ、ㄝ
Between [a] and [i]Jaw slightly open; don't let it blur into [i].
[o]　ㄛ、ㄜ
Round lips, deep mouthLike holding an egg in your mouth; lips too loose turn it into [a].

Build3. Diphthongs and nasal finals

Is the teacher singing 心 (xīn) or 星 (xīng)?

  • The teacher sings 心 or 星 at random, stretching out the end
  • After listening, the whole class holds up 1 or 2 at once, and the teacher counts
  • Press "Clear" for another round, this time with 情 (qíng) and 勤 (qín)

心 closes on the tongue tip (front nasal n); 星 closes on the back of the tongue (back nasal ng). The difference is all in that final closing moment.

Which word did you hear?

BuildEnding a long note

The last moment of a long note decides whether the word is clear

TypeExampleEndHow to sing it
Diphthongs愛 [ai], 熬 [au]Lands on the second vowelHold the first vowel, then close clearly on the second at the end
Front nasal n-an, -en: 心, 勤Tongue tipTongue tip touches lightly just behind the upper teeth
Back nasal ng-ang, -eng: 星, 情Back of the tongueThe back of the tongue rises to touch the soft palate

If the endings of 心 and 星, or 情 and 勤, blur together, people will mishear the lyrics.

Turn01:05–01:40 5. Staccato chord exercises

Triads up and down: 1 3 5 3 1 3 5 3, 1

  • Sing the seven chords of C major in order: C, Dm, Em, F, G, Am, Bdim
  • Push every note out with a bounce from the belly: short, crisp, strong
  • On "in", breathe in quickly and listen for the cue note of the next chord
  • Start at 70 BPM; once the whole class is steady, press "Steady +5 BPM" and go for the class record

TurnConsonants at the microphone

What happens when you sing 怕, 跑, 碰 into a microphone

Consonants rarely stand alone; they mostly show up in fast songs or advertising jingles, like the well-known Taiwanese cold-syrup jingle.

On stage, problems appear when consonants hit the microphone.

Singing the aspirated p t k into a microphone, what's the most common problem?

  1. The sound gets quieter
  2. Popping, a "puh" burst
  3. The pitch drops
  4. No effect at all
The air from aspirated consonants hits the microphone directly and makes a "puh" pop. Ease off on p t k, or angle the microphone slightly away from straight in front of your mouth.
