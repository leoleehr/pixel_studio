Build① The basics of how sound is made

Vibration can be big or small, fast or slow, long or short

Amplitude

Sets the volume: the more force the breath puts on the vocal folds, the bigger the amplitude.

Frequency

Sets the pitch: A4 = 440 Hz, and an octave higher is 880 Hz.

Wavelength

Inversely related to frequency: low notes travel further, which is why the neighbours always hear the bass first.

Build② The basics of how sound is amplified

Your body is a guitar

ElementGuitarVoice
VibrationStringsVocal folds
ResonanceBody, soundholeChest, mouth and throat, head

Guitar experiment

1. Pluck the open strings in order from the sixth to the first.
2. Do it again, stopping each string with your right hand straight after plucking.
3. Press all the strings at the 12th fret with your left index finger and pluck again.
4. Repeat experiment 1 while facing a wall, moving from far to near.

Now try it with your voice: hum the same note in the middle of the room and in a corner, and write down the difference.

Build③ What listeners hear in singing

Hear the pitch clearly and your singing steadies

Pitch: the first thing listeners notice is singing off pitch.

Tone: what lets listeners recognise your voice.

Tempo and rhythm: rushing or dragging both make a song lose its centre.

Rhythm challenge: listen to the demo first, then after the four-beat count-in, sing the same rhythm on "Ta". Orange marks are the target beats, triangles are the beats you sang, and they turn green when you hit. Start with quarter notes, then work up to syncopation and offbeats.

Build③ What listeners hear｜Teacher demo

Teacher demo: Bruno Mars, "I Just Might"

Style in brief｜Retro disco pop blended with funk and soul

Released in January 2026, it went straight to number one on the US Billboard Hot 100 in its first week. Short syncopated electric guitar, a bouncing bass, a horn section of sax, trumpet and trombone, and handclaps drive a mid-tempo groove of about 103 beats per minute.

Pitch

In the repeated chorus line "I just might", notice whether it lands on the same pitch every time. The busier the backing, the more the voice needs to lock onto the chords.

Tone

Compare verse and chorus: which part stays close and speech-like, and which opens up and gets brighter? Notice how the voice matches the brightness of the horns and guitar.

Rhythm

Funk lyrics often land on offbeats and syncopations. Tap along on "Tap tempo" to check it's about 103, then notice when the voice jumps ahead of the beat and when it lands right on it.

Your turn: listen to 〈太陽與地球〉 and choose one answer per row

PitchLong notes in the chorus
ToneVerse into chorus

Listen all the way through once and choose one answer per row; then tap along on "Tap tempo" to check the speed. Finally compare answers with your group and explain your reasons.

Pitch: the ends of the long chorus notes sometimes fall and sometimes lift slightly; both happen.

Tone: going from verse into chorus, the voice opens up and gets brighter.

Tempo: about 66 beats per minute, on the slow side. If you got around 130, you were tapping half beats; just divide by two.

Build⑤ The three basic elements of music

Singing lives inside music

Melody

The main line the singer sings.

Harmony

The colour of the guitar chords, and what your pitch leans on.

Rhythm

The groove and beat of the song.

A question for guitarists: the strumming and fingerpicking patterns you play belong mainly to which element?

  1. Melody
  2. Harmony
  3. Rhythm
  4. Tone
Strumming and fingerpicking patterns set the song's groove, so they belong to rhythm; the chords your left hand presses belong to harmony. The pie chart in the original notes spelled them "Hamonic" and "Rythem"; the correct spellings are Harmony and Rhythm.

Build⑥ What makes a good lead singer

Once you're on stage

Control

Volume, tone and emotion all rise and fall at will.

Stamina

A show has 10 to 15 songs, and the last should sound as good as the first.

Resolution

The resolution of your voice, in other words what makes you you: one line and people know it's you.

Discussion

Where does your favourite singer's recognisability come from? Tone, diction, runs, or a certain emotion? Think on your own first, then swap ideas with the person next to you, and finally share with the class.

The pop song formula

Challenge: arrange the eight sections in the order they first appear in a song.

Intro → Verse → Pre-chorus → Chorus → after the interlude, back to the verse → after the second chorus comes the bridge → back to the chorus → Outro → Ending. A tag often appears in the last chorus, and not every song has a pre-chorus or bridge.

InteractiveRetrieval practice · flip cards
TurnThe role of each section

Guess the role first, then flip the card to see the singing strategy

Verse
The section's job
Tell the story in a mid-to-low rangeClose to a speaking voice, holding back about 30% of the volume.
Pre-chorus
Push the emotion upwardGradually use more air, building energy for the chorus.
Chorus
The song's centre and its hookThe fullest resonance and strongest emotion, usually the highest note too.
Interlude
A rest for the singerReset your breath and get ready for round two.
Bridge
A turn in melody or moodChange tone or intensity so the last chorus hits harder.
Tag
Repeat or extend the last lineYou can change the rhythm or slow down to let it linger.
