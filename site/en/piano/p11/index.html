Build00:20–00:55 Slow rock

12/8: each beat split in three, with a swing to it

How to count

1 & a　2 & a　3 & a　4 & a

Four big beats a bar, twelve eighth notes. The favourite of '50s and '60s rock ballads and oldies.

Pattern

L5 L2 L1　R1 L1 L2

Three notes per beat, two beats per group, with the root on beat one strongest. Take it slower so the triplets stay even.

We met triplets in Lesson 2: one beat split evenly in three. Slow rock uses a triplet groove for the whole song.

BuildThe march and bossa nova

Duple-time "oom-pah", and Brazil's bossa nova

2/4 march

Root and fifth take turns in the bass with the chord in between: oom, pah, oom, pah. Light and lively, good for children's songs and holiday songs.

Bossa Nova

From Brazil, blending samba and jazz, with the bass on beats one and three and the chords played lightly in a syncopated rhythm. Today we just get to know its sound; playing it is outside this course.

The original materials' rhythm styles also include dances such as rumba, cha-cha, tango, disco and boogie-woogie. Pop piano accompaniment mostly comes from traditional dances around the world.

Turn01:05–01:40 Tenth accompaniment

A tenth: the third, an octave up

  • Play the root 1 first, then the fifth 5, then the tenth 10, then rock between 5 and 10
  • Written 1 5 10 5 10 5 10 5, with a wide, gentle sound
  • Richard Clayderman's signature accompaniment in Mariage d'Amour and Ballade pour Adeline
  • If your hands are smaller, play it broken; there's no need to hold the tenth all at once
