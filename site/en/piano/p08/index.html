Build00:20–00:55 Finding a song's key

The first and last chords usually tell you the key

One key

The whole song stays in the same key from start to finish, with no key change. For a beginner playing and singing, choose a song in one key, in C.

How to tell

Look at the first and last chords in the chord chart, then find the three chords that appear most. If C, F, G and Am keep coming up, it's C major.

Song charts often say "Key: C" or "original key E, Capo 4". A piano has no capo, so when you need to, transpose using Lesson 9's scale degrees, or use a digital piano's transpose function.

BuildIntros and outros

Borrow the last four bars of the chorus as the intro

Intro

Play the chords of the chorus's last four bars with the right hand lightly playing the melody, to tell listeners the tempo and key; the intro's last chord often stops on G, ready for the verse.

Outro

Repeat the last line's chords, slowing down gradually; finish with a broken C chord from low to high, holding the sustain pedal so the sound fades naturally.

Play one round of C → Am → F → G in 4 beats, play the G in bar four as a block chord held for two beats, then start singing.

TurnSimplifying the accompaniment and breathing

When singing is a struggle, simplify your hands first

Left hand only

The right hand stops playing the melody; the left plays 4 beats or block chords and the voice carries the melody.

Fewer notes

Go from 8 beats back to 4, and from two hands, two octaves (15th) back to a one-hand octave.

Leave room to breathe

Mark breathing points on the chord chart; on the breath beat, hold the accompaniment or play just the root.

If a song is too high or too low, don't force it. Lower or raise the whole song's key first; Lesson 9 shows how to transpose by scale degree.

Ideas about playing and singing

A song's chord chart starts on C, ends on C, and mostly uses C, F, G and Am. What key is it in?

  1. A minor
  2. G major
  3. C major
  4. F major
It starts and ends on C, and the main chords are all in the C major family.

You keep making mistakes while playing and singing. What's the best way to handle it?

  1. Speed up and get it over with
  2. Simplify the accompaniment and get it steady first
  3. Switch to 8 beats
  4. Stop singing and just play the accompaniment
Cut the accompaniment's notes until your hands don't need to think, then gradually add the original pattern back.
