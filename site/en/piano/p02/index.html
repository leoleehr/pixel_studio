OpenWhat is music?

Three elements of sound: pitch, volume, tone colour

Hz

Pitch: frequency

Vibrations per second. A4 vibrates 440 times a second, which is 440 Hz; human ears hear roughly 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz.

dB

Volume: amplitude

The bigger the vibration, the louder the sound. On the piano, the speed and weight of your touch control the volume.

♪

Tone colour: overtones

At the same 440 Hz, a piano and a flute sound different because of the number and strength of their overtones.

Sound with regular vibration and a fixed pitch is a musical tone; irregular sound with no fixed pitch is noise. The four main elements of music are melody, harmony, rhythm and the tone colour that carries them.

Note names

The letters used in English, fixed names for the keys. Chord names use them too, for example C, Am, G7.

Solfège and numbered notation

Solfège is handy for singing; numbered notation is handy for writing down and showing scale degrees. This course writes melodies in numbered notation: a dot above a number means an octave higher, a dot below an octave lower.

The number after C4 tells you which octave. C to the next C is an octave, and the piano has just over seven.

BuildThe grand staff

The grand staff shows every note's exact position

  • The grand staff stacks the treble staff over the bass staff, the standard layout for piano music
  • Middle C sits between the two staves: the first ledger line below the treble staff
  • The treble staff's first line is E4 and its fifth line F5
  • The bass staff's first line is G2 and its fifth line A3
  • Notes beyond the five lines add ledger lines and spaces above or below

Dot

Adds half the note's value. A dotted half note is 3 beats, a dotted quarter note 1.5 beats.

Triplet

Splits one beat evenly in three; common in slow ballads and slow rock, and we'll meet it again in Lesson 11.

BuildTime signatures

The top is beats per bar, the bottom is which note counts as one beat

2/4・4/4

Duple time

Strong, weak; 4/4 is strong, weak, medium, weak, the most common in pop.

3/4・6/8

Triple time

Strong, weak, weak, like the spin of a waltz; 6/8 is two big beats, each split in three.

5/4・7/8

Mixed meter

Made of twos and threes combined; less common, often found in experimental music.

Clap together as a class: for 4/4 say "1, 2, 3, 4" with beat 1 strongest; for 3/4 say "1, 2, 3", like turning once in a dance.

Turn01:05–01:40 Whole and half steps

Two neighbouring keys are a half step apart

semitones

Two keys right next to each other on the keyboard, black or white. There's no black key between E and F or B and C, so they're a half step apart themselves.

Whole step

Two half steps. There's a black key between C and D, so they're a whole step apart.

Equal temperament: divides the octave evenly into twelve half steps, so every key sounds equally even. Today's pianos are all tuned in equal temperament.

Accidentals written inside a bar last only for that bar; the ones written next to the clef are the key signature and last the whole piece. G major in Lesson 12 has a key signature of one sharp, F♯.

Counting degrees

Count both ends: C to F is C, D, E, F, a fourth.

Why count half steps too

Both are fourths, but C to F is 5 half steps and F to B is 6, so the name changes from perfect fourth to augmented fourth.
