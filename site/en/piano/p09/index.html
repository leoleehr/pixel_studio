OpenThink first

The singer says "too high, bring it down". What does the accompanist do?

C major

C　→　F　→　G　→　C

G major

G　→　C　→　D　→　G

The chord names are completely different, but in their own keys both go degree 1 → degree 4 → degree 5 → degree 1. Remember the degrees and you can play the same song in any key.

Roman numerals mark where a chord sits in the key

DegreeⅠⅡmⅢmⅣⅤⅥmⅦdim
C majorCDmEmFGAmBdim
G majorGAmBmCDEmF♯dim
F majorFGmAmB♭CDmEdim

The diatonic chords of every major key have the same qualities: Ⅰ, Ⅳ and Ⅴ are major, Ⅱ, Ⅲ and Ⅵ minor, Ⅶ diminished.

BuildChord families and functions

The tonic is home, the subdominant goes out, the dominant wants to go home

T

Tonic Ⅰ

Settled, finished. Ⅵm and Ⅲm share two notes with it and can stand in for the tonic.

S

Subdominant Ⅳ

Leaves home and creates movement; Ⅱm is similar and often comes before the dominant.

D

Dominant Ⅴ

Full of tension, wanting most to return to the tonic; Ⅶdim leans the same way.

Ⅰ, Ⅳ and Ⅴ together are the "primary triads": all seven notes of the scale are in these three chords. Lots of folk songs and children's songs need only these three.

BuildCadences

Four cadences: the punctuation at the end of a phrase

Authentic cadencea firm full stop

Ⅴ → Ⅰ (C major: G → C)

The most common and stable cadence, sounding complete, like getting home. With both chords in root position and the melody ending on the tonic Do, it's a perfect authentic cadence, usually at the very end of a piece; with an inversion or the melody not ending on Do, it's an imperfect authentic cadence, like the full stop at the end of a paragraph.

Plagal cadencethe church "Amen"

Ⅳ → Ⅰ (C major: F → C)

Hymns nearly always use this cadence on the final "Amen". Without the strong pull of Ⅴ to Ⅰ, it sounds soft, calm and solemn, second only to the authentic cadence in stability.

Half cadencean unresolved comma

Any chord → Ⅴ (often Ⅰ → Ⅴ, Ⅳ → Ⅴ)

The music stops on the dominant, full of momentum; the sentence isn't finished and listeners wait for the next line. Mostly found in the first half of a phrase, like a question.

Deceptive cadencea surprise turn

Ⅴ → Ⅵm (C major: G → Am), sometimes to Ⅳ

The ear expects to return to Ⅰ, but it turns into a minor chord and the colour darkens at once. Also called an interrupted cadence: the music's energy carries on and the piece has to keep going.

Degrees and functions

What is the Ⅴ chord in G major?

  1. G
  2. C
  3. Em
  4. D
G A B C D: the fifth note is D, so Ⅴ is a D chord.

Which chord most often stands in for the tonic in C major?

  1. Am
  2. F
  3. G
  4. Bdim
Am (A C E) and C (C E G) share C and E, so Am belongs to the tonic family.

Three steps to move a chord chart into another key

01

Write the degrees

C → Am → F → G becomes Ⅰ → Ⅵm → Ⅳ → Ⅴ

02

Choose the new key

The singer finds it too low, so raise it to D major

03

Swap in the new chords

Ⅰ Ⅵm Ⅳ Ⅴ in D major: D → Bm → G → A

The melody's numbered notation doesn't change either: in C, 1 is C; in G, 1 is G. Numbered notation records scale degrees in the first place, which is why pop music uses it so much.
