Build00:20–00:55 Relative major and minor

The sixth degree of a major key is the tonic of its relative minor

majorRelative minorKey signatureHow to find it
C major natural scaleA natural minor scale (Am)No sharps or flatsA minor third down from C is A
G major natural scaleE natural minor scale (Em)One sharp: F♯A minor third down from G is E
F major natural scaleD natural minor scale (Dm)One flat: B♭A minor third down from F is D

You can think of a minor key as another scale "living inside" a major key: they share one key signature and are each other's relative. Move the major key's tonic down a minor third and you have the tonic of its relative minor.

Major and minor, and G major

What's the relative minor of G major?

  1. A minor
  2. E minor
  3. D minor
  4. B minor
The sixth note of G major is E; you can also find E a minor third down from G.

Which note differs between A harmonic minor and A natural minor?

  1. C becomes C♯
  2. F becomes F♯
  3. G becomes G♯
  4. No difference
Harmonic minor raises the seventh a half step: G becomes G♯.
