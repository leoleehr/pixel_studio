Build00:20–00:55 4 beats and 8 beats

One note per beat, or two

4 Beats

L5　L3　L2　L1

Quarter notes, four per bar. Steady and unhurried, good for verses and slow songs.

8 Beats

L5 L3 L2 L1 L5 L3 L2 L1

Eighth notes, eight per bar. Flowing and driving, good for choruses and mid-tempo songs.

One-hand octave

The left hand stays within one octave without moving position; the easiest to pick up.

Two hands, two octaves (15th)

The left hand finishes and hands over to the right, widening the range to two octaves for a broad, rich sound.

BuildHanding over between hands

On the handover beat, the sound mustn't break

  • As the left hand plays L1, the right hand is already hovering over R1
  • The right hand's R1 lands just above the left thumb, without the hands overlapping
  • When the chord changes, the left hand moves to the next root early
  • Press the sustain pedal so the two octaves blend into one

Break it down slowly

Play only L5 L3 L2 L1, then only R1 R2 R5 R2, and finally join them. Add 4 BPM each time it's steady until you reach full speed.

TurnSwitching accompaniment by section

4 beats in the verse, 8 beats in the chorus

Verse

4 beats one-hand octave: few notes, clear lyrics.

Pre-chorus

4 beats two hands, two octaves (15th): the range widens and the mood starts to lift.

Chorus

8 beats two hands, two octaves (15th): the densest notes and widest range, the mood at its peak.

Change pattern only on the last bar of a section, and play the root on the last beat before the change so the ear has somewhere to pause. Keep the tempo the same through the switch, and practise with the metronome on.
