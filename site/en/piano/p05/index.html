Reading order

Say the solfège first, clap the rhythm, find the chords, and only then put it on the keys. Understand it before you play it and you'll learn much faster.

BuildRight-hand melody fingering

Find the melody's lowest and highest notes first, then decide where your hand sits

Five-finger position is enough

Twinkle Twinkle's melody only uses 1 to 6: the right thumb sits on C, finger 5 covers G, and finger 5 reaches over for the sixth note, A.

When the range is more than five notes

For songs with a wider range, move your hand on long notes or rests, or use Lesson 3's thumb-unders and crossings. Once you've settled the fingering, use the same one every time.

Pencilling finger numbers onto the numbered notation is a good pop piano habit. It looks like a chore the first time; by the tenth, your fingers remember on their own.

BuildThree left-hand patterns

From simple to full: root, fifth, block chord

1

Root

Play only the note the chord is named after: C for a C chord, F for an F chord. Left-hand finger 5.

1 5

Fifths

Root plus fifth, for example C and G. Finger 5 and the thumb, steady without getting muddy.

1 3 5

Block chords

All three notes pressed together, left-hand fingering 5 3 1. Careful not to play louder than the right-hand melody.

The original materials' teaching order: get the left hand comfortable with fifths first, then add right-hand octave fingering. Fifths are the most common left-hand foundation in pop piano.

Turn01:05–01:40 Chord tones and choosing chords

Choose the chord that the melody note belongs to

ChordChord tonesMelody notes that fit
C（Ⅰ）1 3 5Do、Mi、Sol
F（Ⅳ）4 6 1Fa、La、Do
G（Ⅴ）5 7 2Sol、Si、Re

Watch the strong beats

The melody note on beat one of each bar matters most; choose the chord by that first.

End back on C

Phrases often end G → C, which sounds like coming home. Sol is in both C and G, so either works.

Choose chords for a melody

The melody's first beat is La (6). Which chord fits best?

  1. C
  2. F
  3. G
  4. None of them
An F chord is 4 6 1, and La is its third.

The melody is 2 2 1, ending back on Do. What's the most common chord for this bar?

  1. F → C
  2. C → F
  3. G → C
  4. C → G
Re is in the G chord; switch to C when it returns to Do, for a "dominant to tonic" cadence.
