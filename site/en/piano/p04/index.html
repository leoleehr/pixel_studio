OpenHarmony and chords

Two or more notes make harmony; three notes stacked in thirds make a chord

Harmony

The sound of two or more notes at once. Lesson 2's intervals are the smallest harmony.

Chord

Three or more notes stacked a third apart. Start on Do, add Mi and Sol, and you have a C chord: 1 3 5.

Chords are the colour of pop music. One unusual chord can give a listener a completely different feeling; learn chords and you can accompany any song from a chord chart.

Build00:20–00:55 Four kinds of triad

Major and minor thirds combine into four colours

ChordBuilt fromOn CSymbolSound
Major triadMajor third + minor thirdC E GCBright, open
Minor triadMinor third + major thirdC E♭ GCmSoft, sad
Augmented triadMajor third + major thirdC E G♯C+、CaugSuspenseful, uneasy
Diminished triadMinor third + minor thirdC E♭ G♭Cdim、C°Tense, eerie

Root, third, fifth: the bottom note is the root and gives the chord its name; the third in the middle decides major or minor; the fifth on top decides augmented or diminished.

  1. D F♯ A
  2. D F A
  3. D F A♭

A minor third D→F, then a major third F→A.

Lower the third of a C chord, E, by a half step and you get?

  1. Cm
  2. Cdim
  3. Caug
  4. Csus4
The third decides major or minor: C E G becomes C E♭ G, a C minor triad.

Turn01:05–01:40 Inversions

Same three notes, a different note at the bottom

C E G

Root position

Root at the bottom, the most stable. Right-hand fingering 1 3 5.

E G C

First inversion

Third at the bottom, a lighter sound. Right-hand fingering 1 2 5.

G C E

Second inversion

Fifth at the bottom, often used to lead into the next chord. Right-hand fingering 1 3 5.

Close position: the three notes packed within an octave; open position: the notes spread apart for a wider sound. Lesson 11's tenth accompaniment grows out of open position.

TurnWhat inversions are for

Inversions keep the right hand from moving much and join chords more smoothly

Three arrangements of the same C chord
Root positionC E G (root at the bottom)
First inversionE G C (third at the bottom)
Second inversionG C E (fifth at the bottom)
ChordAll root position (the hand has to jump)With inversions (the hand barely moves)
CC E G (start in root position)
FF A CC F A (second inversion)
GG B DB D G (first inversion)
CC E G (back to root position)

Common notes stay put and the others move to the nearest note. This is called "nearest-note voice leading", and nearly every right-hand chord in pop piano is played this way.

TurnIdeas about chords

Myth or fact?

When you invert a chord, its name changes too
MythThe notes stay the same, so an inverted C chord is still a C chord.
The third decides whether a chord is major or minor
FactA major third from root to third makes a major chord; a minor third makes a minor chord.
There are no minor chords in C major
MythDm, Em and Am are all in C major and can be played on white keys alone.
Augmented and diminished chords both sound uneasy
FactBoth contain a dissonant augmented or diminished fifth and are often used to build tension.
A chord always has to be pressed with three fingers together
MythChords can be played apart too; that's the broken-chord accompaniment we start in Lesson 6.
The chord symbol C+ means an augmented triad
FactC+ is the same as Caug; a diminished triad is written C° or Cdim.
